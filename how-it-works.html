01

Read this first

This is a demonstration build, not a product. The decision logic is real and runs in the browser; everything that would leave the browser in production is simulated on screen. Throughout this page and inside the tool, one convention holds:

Solid — logic that really runs in the BETA
Dotted — simulated: the payload is shown, nothing is sent
Cyan — the part of the mechanism under discussion
Real

Product references, the compatibility and composition rules, the room-by-room logic, the generated system diagram, the savings method, the project resume, the CRM payloads.

Fictional but labelled

Every price (a “fictional price” tag sits next to it), the savings coefficients, and every PDF — watermarked TEST DOCUMENT.

Not connected

Zoho CRM, emails and SMS, the pre-visit link, the installer directory. The “CRM backstage” panel shows exactly what would go out. Nothing leaves the browser.

02

The one idea: the questionnaire never names a product

Every earlier configurator asked the customer to pick from a catalogue. This one asks about the home, turns the answers into a system described in roles (“one head per radiator”, “one actuator per loop”), and only resolves those roles into product codes at the last possible moment. That seam is what makes the rest possible.

03

One journey, one exit for humans, three side doors

There is a single path, four named steps, and a bar that is always visible so nobody wonders how far they are. Going back loses nothing. The two questions customers get wrong on their own — “do I need connected?” and “what will it cost?” — are never asked: one becomes a conclusion, the other appears as a live total from step 2.

04

One configuration, every output

The “My solution” screen is not a page of results, it is eight views of the same object. Nobody re-enters anything to get a diagram, a quote or an installation guide — which is precisely why the price, the guide and the drawing can never disagree with each other.

05

The loop that makes the tool get better

This is the part with the most commercial value and the least code: a configuration is a qualified lead the moment it starts, and every correction a sales rep makes to a handed-over file is a rule the engine is missing. Simulated end to end in the BETA — the payloads are visible in the “CRM backstage” panel.

06

The 26 problems, and where each one is answered

Every problem from the workshop, grouped by the part of the mechanism that answers it — so you can point at a diagram above rather than describe a feature. The status says how much of the answer really runs today.

A — The engine decides, so the customer doesn't have to 6 problems · diagrams 02

These six are all answered by the same thing: the seam between roles and the catalogue, plus the three-state check on top of it.

P1Live

Identifying all compatible devices

Three states instead of a yes/no: compatible, compatible with a stated limitation, or greyed with the reason in one sentence and the missing product on an add button. A completeness pass checks the whole basket.

P2Live

Matching needs with available devices

Answers fill attributes, attributes compose roles, roles resolve into products last. No catalogue is shown before the recommendation.

P4Live

Customers unsure which devices suit them

Nobody picks devices. The tool proposes Essential / Comfort / Premium, computed from composition rules: 0 thermostats, 1 in the main living room, or 1 per room.

P6Live

Seeing and understanding all the options

The result screen shows the home room by room on top and the list below, with “Other choice” on every line — same role, compatible, price and function difference in one line.

P10Live

The system may not suit the location

Walls, floors, area and a wi-fi proxy question feed radio-range rules: an RE600 repeater is added by default with the reason, and can be removed. A warning, never a block.

P24Live

The right system within a budget

A budget slider. A level above budget stays visible and greyed with the gap shown rather than disappearing — and packs are never mixed into the levels to make one fit.

D — The installer's own tools 4 problems · diagrams 03 & 04

The installer is a second user with different problems: getting to site prepared, finding the one document that matters, and turning a configuration into something a customer can sign.

P8Simulated

Having to be on site to specify

A pre-visit link goes to the customer, who answers the homeowner questions and sends three photos asked for explicitly: heat source, radiator with its valve, electrical panel. The installer arrives with the configuration pre-filled and priced.

Fictional link and simulated customer reply.

P13Test content

Drowning in documentation

The documentation matrix is queried with the selected products only: one block per product, a missing document shown as missing, the full pack in one click.

Placeholder PDFs.

P14Live

Finding a replacement for an existing device

Typo-tolerant search covering competitors — Delta Dore, Netatmo, Honeywell, Tado — plus legacy Salus, listing the additional products the swap needs.

Table limited to 10 entries; the label photo is simulated. A search that comes back empty is what enriches the table.

P25Test content

Turning a configuration into a quote

A quote at recommended retail price with the exact product codes, plus the message “present this quote to your Salus distributor” — no net price in the tool, so the distributor's role is protected and nothing is retyped.

Test PDF, simulated national numbering.

E — When the tool can't decide, and how it learns 3 problems · diagrams 03 & 05

The three problems that are not about the interface at all. They are the reason the configurator has commercial value beyond the demo.

P9Simulated

No single supplier for everything

The postcode routes the project to the area sales rep, with a clear message: this is a service, not a dead end. The rep points it at the distributor able to supply the whole system.

Payload visible in the backstage panel.

P15Simulated

Market configurators are too limited

Instead of refusing an out-of-scope project, the tool prepares a qualified file with its partial recommendation, its assumptions and the points to check, and promises a call back in 48 h. Every human correction becomes a rule to add.

Zoho Deals simulated.

P26Simulated

Not connected to the CRM / data lake

Every configuration creates or updates a Lead — profile, postcode, project, zones, level, amount, and the step where the customer dropped off — which is what makes retargeting and the 48 h reminder possible.

Fully simulated by design: the “CRM backstage” panel shows the exact payloads, the records created and what follows for the rep.

07

What a demo must not claim

If you show this to anyone outside the team, these are the lines to hold. The tool labels all of it on screen, but it is easier to say it before someone asks.

What is actually blocking, in order

  1. Four product references to validate — TRV3RF-AB (auto-balancing head), T30NC (loop actuator), THB (auto-balancing actuator), MS600 (presence sensor). Everything else comes from the existing Salus configurators.
  2. Visuals — 64 placeholders are in place at final dimensions and swap in by dropping a file, with no code change. Highest impact first: the four situation images and the three landing visuals.
  3. Two assets with no placeholder at all — the vector icon library for the system diagram (plus around ten reference drawings) and the videos.
  4. One product decision to confirm — the Premium level for underfloor heating currently adds auto-balancing actuators. That is an interpretation: the workshop rule only mentioned TRVs.
The five-minute version. Open the demo, click “Demo scenario — mixed house” on the landing page: underfloor downstairs, radiators upstairs, gas boiler, pre-filled in one click. Then walk the result screen top to bottom — levels, rooms, diagram, documents — and finish in the CRM backstage panel. That single path touches every problem in section 06.
